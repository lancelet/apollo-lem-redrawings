About this redraw

This is a redraw of Grumman drawing LDW280-17619, sheet 3, a detail drawing of a flat tapered link from the Apollo Lunar Excursion Module (the Lunar Module). It has been rebuilt as a fully constrained drawing: every line, arc, dimension and piece of lettering is tied to evidence in the original scan, and you can compare the two side by side.

Where the original gives a dimension, the redraw uses it. Where the original does not, the redraw says so: values we inferred are marked as inferred, in their own colour, with the basis for them.

How to read it

  • Hover (or tap, on a phone) over a line or a piece of text to light it in the redraw and the ink it comes from in the scan. A dimension lights with its extension lines, arrowheads and value.
  • Click (or tap) to pin it. A pinned item shows its connections: the constraints that tie it to the rest of the drawing. Tap empty paper to let go.
  • Hold Shift while hovering to see the connections without pinning. Hold Alt (or long-press on a phone) to take a single piece instead of its whole group.
  • Details (the chip at the bottom, or key i) opens what we know about the pinned item: what it is, its geometry, the constraints on it, the evidence and where it came from, back to the archive.
  • Pan with a drag or two-finger scroll; zoom with a pinch or Command + scroll; press 0 (or double-click) to fit the sheet.

The source and the rights

The scan is of an aperture card in box 511 of the Grumman Lunar Module drawings held by the National Archives at Fort Worth (Southwest region). It was uploaded to the Internet Archive by the Virtual AGC Project, in the item apertureCardBox511Part1NARASW_images, where it is marked with the Creative Commons Public Domain Mark 1.0. That is the uploader's statement; we have not checked it independently.

The drawing was created by the Grumman Aircraft Engineering Corporation. The scan shown here is cropped to the drawing from the archive's original image, which you can fetch from the item (the paper trail in an item's Details gives the exact address and checksum).

The redraw, and the way it is tied to the scan, are new work. The code is licensed under MIT or Apache-2.0, at your option. The drawings and scans carry the Public Domain Mark as stated above.
